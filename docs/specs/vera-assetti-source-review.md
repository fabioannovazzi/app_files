## Implementation and evaluation notes

Start with the skill method and source references. Assess whether existing
observations/findings/actions can express the richer analysis before adding
record fields. Add structured fields only when required for reliable delivery
or follow-up. Preserve source bindings and explicit professional decisions.

Candidate evaluation cases should include:

1. A small owner-managed firm with functioning informal controls: recognize
   corroborated operation without inventing large-company departments.
2. Complete written procedures but no use: do not treat documentation as proof
   of functioning.
3. Timely reports with no management response: distinguish information
   production from decision and action.
4. A retrospective review: later losses must not become information available
   to management at an earlier date.
5. A cooperative with social lending and multiple mutualistic activities:
   include relevant questions; do not ask them of an ordinary company.
6. A signal calculation with missing inputs or an omitted statutory condition:
   surface the limitation and distinguish internal triggers from legal signals.
7. An article's model minutes containing favorable declarations: leave them
   unsupported unless the company evidence establishes them.
8. A proposed action with a new policy but no operating evidence: do not conclude
   effective implementation solely from policy adoption.

These are proposed acceptance scenarios, not tests already executed. Semantic
quality requires review of generated assessments against representative evidence;
string checks and schema validation alone do not establish improvement.
